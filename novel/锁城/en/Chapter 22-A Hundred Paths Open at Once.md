# Chapter 22: A Hundred Paths Open at Once

Nova saw everything from the communications tower.

One hundred twenty small screens running simultaneously—conventional communication channels, Heap District data flows, Old Generation status, Metadata Attic bottom-layer cleanup progress, ripple interference effects in direct memory. Information from every path flooded in like a deluge.

But Nova didn't panic. This was her gift—watching a hundred paths at once.

"Cole's charging toward the south segment." She reported into her comm. "Device full-volume loading progress—three percent. Estimated completion—four minutes thirty seconds."

Four and a half minutes.

In four and a half minutes, Cole had to cross Segment Three's corridors, reach the device's location, and shut it down while Donovan tried to stop him.

"Corridor status—the south segment's main passage is clear, but three data cables span overhead. The cables' transmission frequency is very high—if Cole runs straight through, the data cables might interfere with him."

"What kind of interference?" Cole's voice came through the comm, slightly breathless.

"The cables are transmitting type definitions—ones currently being loaded. If a type definition in transit contacts a thread person, it temporarily alters some of the thread person's attributes."

"Alters what?"

"Uncertain. Depends on the type definition content. Could be minor—changing your ID number's last digit. Could be significant—changing your execution priority."

Cole didn't slow down. "How much time do I have?"

"At your current speed, reaching the device takes a minute and a half. Full-volume loading finishes in four minutes. Time left for you and Donovan—two and a half minutes."

Enough. Or not enough.

Cole plunged into the south segment's main corridor. The data cables along the walls were denser than the north side—light flowing fast enough to dazzle. The three cables spanning overhead blazed with a blinding radiance, like three laser curtains.

Cole didn't hesitate. He ducked under the first cable.

A bolt of electric pain shot from his scalp through his entire body. His vision flickered—like someone turned a page before his eyes.

The first type definition had passed through his body. He didn't know what attribute had changed—maybe the last digit of his ID, maybe his priority had dropped. But it didn't affect his movement.

The second cable. He hunched low and squeezed under.

This time the pain was sharper. He felt his reaction speed dip slightly—his perception of time stretched. Not that he'd slowed, but his timescale had been elongated.

The effect of the second type definition.

The third cable.

Cole charged straight through. No pain this time—just a cold numbness. His left hand lost all sensation for several seconds.

But he kept running.

"Through the third cable." Nova reported. "Your left hand should be temporarily numb. Estimated recovery—thirty seconds."

"Don't care." Cole said. "Where's the device?"

"Fifty meters ahead. Turn left."

Cole turned left and burst into the device's chamber.

Donovan stood beside the device. Both hands pressed flat against the control panel, every ounce of his strength sustaining the full-volume loading.

"Cole." Donovan didn't turn around. "You've arrived."

"Shut it down."

"Can't." Donovan said. "Once full-volume loading starts, it can't be interrupted. It's the class-loading device's design—once loading begins, it must finish before it stops."

"Then I'll destroy it."

"Destroying the device triggers cascading collapse." Donovan said. "Meta told you that."

"Meta also told me—cascading collapse presupposes that all malicious types are still active." Cole said. "But your reference maintenance has stopped. Warden's ripple interference is ongoing. Meta's concurrent cleanup is in progress. Your malicious types are failing one after another."

He stepped up beside the device, face to face with Donovan.

"If I destroy the device right now, the already-failed types won't cause cascading collapse—because they've already failed. The types still running will be affected, but there aren't many left."

"You're sure?" A note of mockery in Donovan's voice. "Can you guarantee no cascading reaction whatsoever?"

"No guarantee." Cole said. "But full-volume loading completing—guarantees Full GC. Destroying the device—only has a partial chance of problems."

"You're gambling."

"Thirty percent." Cole said. "I said that from the start."

Donovan looked at him.

"Are you really willing to gamble the entire city on thirty percent?"

"Not gambling the entire city." Cole said. "Gambling my life. If I lose, I stay beside the device—the first victim of cascading collapse will be me. It won't spread far."

Donovan's eyes widened slightly.

"What are you talking about?"

"I'm saying—" Cole grabbed the device's core conduit. The pipe was scorching hot; his palm sizzled with the sound of burning, "if the device blows, the first thing that goes is this pipe I'm gripping. The data stream inside will flood straight into my body—my stack, my heap, my reference relationships—all destroyed. But I'm just one thread person. One thread person's collapse won't trigger a citywide cascade."

"You'll die."

"I know."

Donovan's lips moved, but no words came out.

"Nova." Cole said into his comm. "Full-volume loading progress?"

"Eighteen percent."

"Meta's cleanup progress?"

"Malicious types cleared—sixty-two percent. Thirty-eight percent still running."

"If I destroy the device—"

"Those thirty-eight percent will fail simultaneously. About twenty percent of those will exhibit abnormal behavior. The abnormal behavior's impact range—" Nova calculated, "affects the entire Old Generation. But won't spread to the New Generation or the commercial strip."

"Death toll?"

Nova was silent for a second.

"If the impact is limited to the Old Generation—three at most."

Three.

Fewer than eleven.

Far fewer than hundreds.

Cole tightened his grip on the conduit.

"Cole." Donovan's voice suddenly shifted—no longer the tone of an obstacle, but something more complex. "You—"

"Step aside." Cole said.

Donovan didn't step aside. He looked at Cole's face—that silent, taut, prepared young face.

"Are you truly not afraid?" Donovan asked.

"I'm afraid." Cole said. "But Full GC is worse."

His hand applied force.

The conduit warped in his grip. The data stream emitted a piercing shriek.

The device's lens spun to its limit—and then shattered.

Fragments scattered. Data erupted from the ruptured conduit like a dam bursting.

Cole's body took the full brunt of the data stream.

He felt everything being torn apart—stack frames collapsing, references snapping, his identity code dissolving.

But the data stream didn't pour entirely into his body as he'd expected.

Someone had stepped in front of him.

Donovan.

Donovan used his body to block the lower half of the data stream. The stream split into two—one pouring into Cole, one into Donovan.

"You—" Cole's eyes went wide.

"Did you think you were the only one who isn't afraid to die?" Donovan's voice was barely audible over the shriek of data. "My commercial zone. Sixteen segments. My territory. If Lockhaven falls—my segments fall too."

His gray-white hair scattered in the data stream. His robe was shredded to ribbons.

"You're insane." Cole said.

"Aren't you?" Donovan gave a bitter smile.

The data stream's onslaught lasted fifteen seconds.

Then it stopped.

The device went completely dark. Segment Three plunged into blackness.

Cole dropped to his knees. His palms were scorched black, his left arm numb. But he was alive.

Donovan lay beside him.

Donovan's condition was far worse. The data stream had struck his core—his stack was almost entirely destroyed. His eyes still moved, but his body couldn't.

"Donovan." Cole crawled to his side.

"Don't—don't touch me." Donovan's voice was hoarse as shattered glass. "I can—hold on a little longer."

"You shouldn't have—"

"Shut up." A last glimmer shone in Donovan's eyes. "Let me tell you something."

"What?"

"The platform-level token—I used it for three years. In those three years I injected—over seventeen hundred incremental updates into the Attic's bottom layer." Donovan's breathing grew shallower. "Can Meta's concurrent cleanup—finish them all?"

Cole checked his comm. Nova's face was pale.

"Seventy-eight percent cleared." Nova said. "Still continuing."

Donovan closed his eyes.

"The rest—Cole—you go clean up."

"How?"

"The token—it's still on me." Donovan's voice was barely audible. "Token's unbound—but not gone. Before I disappear—it's still valid. Use my token—revoke all remaining increments."

"Your token—how can I use it?"

"You're a—thread person. Thread people—can hold—tokens." Donovan's hand moved with immense effort, and a silver badge rolled from the tatters of his robe—the platform-level token.

"Take it." Donovan said. "Go to the Attic—clean up—the rest."

Cole reached out and picked up the token.

Its surface was cold. But beneath the cold, Cole sensed a faint warmth—Donovan's residual identity information. The token still recognized him.

"Donovan—"

"Don't say my name." Donovan's voice faded further. "I'm just—a man who made a mistake."

His eyes closed.

His body began to turn transparent.

Cole watched Donovan's silhouette gradually dissolve. In the last moment before the man vanished, his lips seemed to move.

Cole didn't catch what he said.

Then the space was empty.

Cole clenched the token in his hand.

He stood up. Agony throughout his body, but he could still move.

"Nova." His voice was hoarse. "Device destroyed. Full-volume loading terminated."

"I see it." Nova's voice trembled. "Old Generation data flows—diminishing. No new types being injected."

"Meta's cleanup progress?"

"Eighty-five percent. Continuing."

"Warden?"

"Ripple interference still sustained. Hidden channel bandwidth in direct memory has dropped to near zero."

Cole took a deep breath.

"Tell Meta—I'm carrying Donovan's token. Requesting direct access to the Attic's bottom layer to revoke remaining incremental updates."

Two seconds of silence on the comm.

Then Meta's voice came through—cool, but with something more than before.

"Come up." She said.

Cole gripped the token and walked out of Segment Three.

Behind him, the device's wreckage sat silent in the darkness.

On the floor was nothing but a gray-white circular mark—the spot where Donovan had stood.

Cole didn't look back.
