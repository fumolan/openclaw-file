# Lockhaven

## Genre
Cyberpunk × Java Concurrency

## World

Lockhaven is a cyberpunk metropolis shrouded by a colossal data architecture called the "Heap." Every tier of the city is a memory region — the labyrinthine "Stack Alley" at the bottom, the bustling "Heap District" commercial belt in the middle, and the cold, efficient "Metadata Attic" at the summit. The city runs on millions of "thread folk" — its labor force, threading through every district to execute tasks.

Lockhaven has a deadly cycle: at irregular intervals, the "Sweepers" launch a city-wide purge. All thread folk are forced to stop dead in their tracks while the Sweepers mark and reclaim abandoned zones that have lost their references. This process is called the "Pause." Every pause halts the city's heartbeat. People die in pauses. People vanish in pauses.

Order in Lockhaven is maintained by seven major factions, each controlling critical passages and resources. The city's core contradiction is simple: passages are finite, but thread folk multiply endlessly. When two thread folk squeeze into the same narrow alley, who goes first? When a crowd swarms a single resource point, who earns the right to take it?

The answers lie in Lockhaven's rules. And the rules are falling apart.

## Main Characters

1. **Cole** (male, 28) — The city's finest passage controller, capable of regulating traffic through any corridor with surgical precision. Quiet and terse, he solves problems in as few words as possible. Catchphrase: "Lock it down. We'll talk later."
2. **Jace** (male, 32) — Cole's partner, a master of CAS (Compare-And-Swap) techniques who can perform pinpoint operations without any locks whatsoever. Brash and cocky, he talks big but delivers every time. Catchphrase: "Let me try first. No lock needed."
3. **Vera** (female, 26) — An information broker in the Heap District who knows the city's memory layout like the back of her hand. Glamorous exterior, sharp tongue, fiercely loyal to friends. She can tell at a glance whether an object is still referenced or has been abandoned.
4. **Slater** (male, 45) — "The Pool Master," manager of the thread pool. He keeps twenty permanent workers on staff, hires temps when demand spikes, and cuts them loose when things slow down. Shrewd and calculating, every word he speaks is measured against cost. Catchphrase: "My workers aren't free."
5. **Donovan** (male, 55) — Patriarch of the segmented lock faction, controlling Lockhaven's busiest commercial zone. He divides his district into sixteen segments, each managed independently. Deeply cunning and inscrutable, he speaks in circles. Catchphrase: "Mind your own segment. Don't cross the line."
6. **Meta** (female, 30) — Guardian of the Metadata Attic, holding the keys to all type information in the city. Cold and aloof, she rarely speaks — but when she does, it's always critical. She is the only one who knows Lockhaven's "class loading" secret.
7. **Gordon** (male, 38) — Keeper of the thread-safe queues, responsible for the orderly flow of goods through the Heap District. Gentle but stubborn, he insists on absolute first-in-first-out order and never allows anyone to cut in line. Catchphrase: "Line up. One at a time."
8. **Rhea** (female, 24) — Twin sister of the read-write lock pair, managing the "Read Passage." Open and generous, she shares information freely but despises tampering. Catchphrase: "Look all you want. Just don't touch."
9. **Wyatt** (male, 24) — Twin brother of the read-write lock pair, managing the "Write Passage." Sullen and paranoid, he allows no one near when he's working. Catchphrase: "I'm making changes. Everyone back off."
10. **Grant** (male, 40) — Lockhaven's "Container Master," overseeing the safety of all data containers in the city. His "segmented container" design is one of Lockhaven's greatest inventions — dividing containers into independent segments, each with its own lock, massively improving concurrency. Steady and reliable, he's the technical backbone everyone trusts.
11. **Quincy** (male, 50) — The most mysterious figure in Lockhaven, known as "The Fair One." He maintains an underlying coordination mechanism called "AQS" — all locks, barriers, and signals ultimately depend on the queuing rules he established. Strictly impartial, he never favors anyone. Catchphrase: "The queuing rules aren't mine. They belong to everyone."
12. **Nova** (female, 22) — A new-generation thread folk with NIO (New IO) talent, capable of monitoring multiple channels simultaneously. Curious and impulsive, she always wants to do more things at once. Catchphrase: "Why watch only one path? I can watch a hundred."
13. **Warden** (male, 60) — Leader of the Sweepers, responsible for the city's garbage collection. His sweeping methods have evolved over the years — from primitive serial sweeping to concurrent mark-sweep, to the latest region-based collection. But deep inside, he harbors a secret: every sweep, he hesitates.

## Core Settings (Technical Embodiments)

1. **Lock System**: Lockhaven's passage control tools — biased locks (fast single-person passage markers), lightweight locks (rotating turnstiles for mild competition), heavyweight locks (fully sealed checkpoints for intense contention)
2. **Thread Pool**: Slater's workforce — permanent core workers, temps hired on demand, idle workers cut loose after timeout
3. **CAS Operations**: Jace's signature technique — comparing current state against memory, updating if unchanged, retrying if changed, all without any lock
4. **Memory Regions**: The Heap District's physical structure — Stack Alley (thread-private), Heap commercial belt (shared), Metadata Attic (type information storage)
5. **Garbage Collection**: The Sweepers' purge operations — young generation quick sweeps, old generation deep marking, city-wide Pause as the ultimate cleansing
6. **Segmented Containers**: Grant's core data infrastructure — splitting massive containers into independent segments, each operating autonomously without blocking others
7. **Class Loading**: Meta's guarded secret — the three-stage ritual for loading type information into the city (loading → linking → initializing), and the parent-delegation inheritance rules
8. **NIO Multiplexing**: Nova's gift — monitoring hundreds of channels' readiness states through a single dispatch console
9. **AQS Queuing**: Quincy's underlying coordination rules — FIFO queues on a first-come-first-served basis, all thread folk must queue when competing for resources
10. **Happen-Before**: Lockhaven's temporal law — certain operation results must be visible to others, the fundamental causality governing the city

## Plot Summary

Lockhaven faces an unprecedented crisis — pause times are skyrocketing. Warden the Sweeper finds his purges increasingly frequent, trapping the city in a vicious cycle. Cole and Jace are drawn into the investigation, discovering the root cause: massive "leaked objects" in the Heap District's old generation — abandoned zones that should have been cleared but inexplicably maintain their references, making them uncollectable.

Vera helps track the clues through the Heap, revealing a deeper conspiracy: Donovan's segmented lock faction is modifying class loading rules to inject external forces into the city's core. Meta holds crucial evidence but can't reveal it directly due to her parent-delegation oath.

Nova uses her NIO gift to uncover hidden channels, while Slater's thread pool becomes the backbone of the counteroffensive. The final showdown takes place in the Metadata Attic — Cole must deploy every lock technique at his disposal, coordinating with Jace's CAS operations and Quincy's queuing mechanism, to purge the corrupted type information and restore order before the Sweepers trigger a city-wide Pause.

## Chapter Plan (30 Chapters)

### Part One: The City of Pauses (Chapters 1–10)

**Chapter 1 — The Pause at Three in the Morning**
Cole is patrolling the Heap District commercial belt when the city suddenly pauses. All thread folk are frozen in place as the Sweepers deploy. Cole watches a thread folk vanish before his eyes — too late to stop it. After the pause ends, he discovers the vanished aren't alone. He decides to investigate. Technical embodiment: GC pause (STW), thread freezing. Cliffhanger: Why are pauses becoming more frequent?

**Chapter 2 — The Old Lock in the Alley**
Cole returns to Stack Alley and finds his biased lock has been tampered with. The lock, which should recognize only him, shows signs of another presence. He and Jace analyze the lock's state — the biased lock has been revoked, upgraded to a lightweight lock. Technical embodiment: Biased lock revocation, lock upgrade. Cliffhanger: Who touched Cole's biased lock?

**Chapter 3 — Twenty Permanent Workers**
Cole and Jace visit Slater to borrow thread pool resources for their investigation. Slater drives a hard bargain, demanding they first solve his problem — he's running out of workers. Tasks have surged, all core workers are busy, temps keep getting hired but can't keep up. Technical embodiment: Thread pool core size, temp thread expansion, task queue backlog. Cliffhanger: Why did tasks suddenly spike?

**Chapter 4 — Undercurrents in the Heap**
Vera's intelligence network picks up anomalous signals — massive "objects" in the old generation keep growing but are never collected. She traces their reference chains to a single source, but the chain's endpoint is obscured by a strange barrier. Technical embodiment: Memory leaks, object reference chain tracing, old generation bloat. Cliffhanger: What's at the end of the reference chain?

**Chapter 5 — Winning Without Locks**
Jace encounters a tricky situation — a shared counter needs precise updates but has no locks. He demonstrates his CAS technique: read current value, calculate new value, compare — if unchanged, write; if changed, retry. He retries seventeen times before succeeding. Technical embodiment: CAS operations, spin retry, ABA problem. Cliffhanger: Can CAS ever fail?

**Chapter 6 — The People in Line**
Cole follows clues to Quincy's territory — the AQS queuing zone. Hundreds of thread folk wait in FIFO queues. Cole notices the queues are longer than usual and spotted unfamiliar faces — seemingly inserted from outside. Technical embodiment: AQS FIFO wait queues, fair vs. unfair competition. Cliffhanger: Who are those unfamiliar faces?

**Chapter 7 — Mind Your Own Segment**
Donovan invites Cole to his commercial zone. Divided into sixteen segments with independent passage controls. Donovan hints he knows the truth behind the pauses but demands Cole join his faction. Cole refuses. On his way out, he notices Segment 3's lock level is abnormal — far more complex than others. Technical embodiment: ConcurrentHashMap's segmented lock mechanism, segment-level independent control. Cliffhanger: What's hidden in Segment 3?

**Chapter 8 — Messages on the Shelf**
Gordon's supply queue is blocked — packages pile up, unable to dequeue. Cole helps investigate and finds the queue's head is stuck on a task that will never complete. Gordon insists on FIFO, refusing to skip the blocked task. Technical embodiment: BlockingQueue's take/put operations, head-of-line blocking, unbounded queue risks. Cliffhanger: Who placed that never-completing task?

**Chapter 9 — Look All You Want, Just Don't Touch**
Rhea shows Cole her "Read Passage" — an information corridor allowing many through simultaneously. She's proud that a hundred readers at once is no problem — as long as no one modifies data. But when Wyatt appears at the corridor's end, everyone is forced out. Wyatt is modifying data — no reading allowed. Technical embodiment: Read-write lock's concurrent read, exclusive write. Cliffhanger: What data is Wyatt modifying?

**Chapter 10 — The Secret of the Metadata Attic**
Cole finally meets Meta. She reveals Lockhaven's secret — type information isn't innate but loaded through a three-stage ritual from outside: read raw data, verify and link, then initialize. Loading authority follows "parent delegation" rules — ask the parent first, only load yourself if the parent can't. Meta hints someone has bypassed this. Technical embodiment: Class loading three stages, parent-delegation model. Cliffhanger: Who bypassed parent delegation?

### Part Two: The Source of the Leak (Chapters 11–20)

**Chapter 11 — The People Not on the List**
Jace uses CAS to audit the population counter and discovers 347 more thread folk than registered. These unlisted individuals roam the Heap, holding references to objects that the Sweepers can't reclaim. Technical embodiment: Thread leaks, object reference holding, GC Roots reachability analysis. Cliffhanger: Where did the extra people come from?

**Chapter 12 — Why Watch Only One Path?**
Nova's first appearance — monitoring 120 channels simultaneously from a communications tower. Cole can't understand how she does it. She shows her gift: a dispatch console polling all channels, handling whichever is ready first. But she admits she can't guarantee processing order. Technical embodiment: NIO Selector multiplexing, non-blocking IO, readiness polling. Cliffhanger: What consequences will out-of-order processing bring?

**Chapter 13 — The Old Generation's Secret**
Vera penetrates the old generation and finds it nearing capacity. More诡异 still, some objects have references pointing to nonexistent addresses — as if pointing somewhere beyond the city. Technical embodiment: Object promotion from young to old generation, old generation space shortage, anomalous references. Cliffhanger: What's beyond the city?

**Chapter 14 — The First Purge**
The Sweepers launch a deep mark-sweep of the old generation. Warden personally leads the operation. But after sweeping, little space is freed — fragmentation is severe. Technical embodiment: Mark-sweep algorithm, memory fragmentation. Cliffhanger: How to deal with fragmented space?

**Chapter 15 — The Sweeper's Evolution**
Warden shows Cole the evolution of sweeping methods — from serial to concurrent mark-sweep to region-based collection. But none solve the fundamental problem: objects held by anomalous references can't be touched. Technical embodiment: GC evolution (Serial → CMS → G1), algorithm comparison. Cliffhanger: Who's protecting the anomalous objects?

**Chapter 16 — The Truth of Segment 3**
Cole infiltrates Donovan's Segment 3 alone. The passage is locked with a heavyweight lock — extreme competition inside. He discovers an illegal custom class loader running, continuously injecting new type information into the city. Technical embodiment: Heavyweight lock contention, custom class loaders, dynamic class loading. Cliffhanger: What are these loaded types?

**Chapter 17 — The Rules of the Queue Have Changed**
Quincy discovers anomalies in his queuing zone — new arrivals skip the line and grab resources directly. He realizes this is unfair competition; his fair rules are being undermined. Cole and Quincy trace the line-jumpers — all from Segment 3. Technical embodiment: AQS fair vs. unfair modes, unfair lock "cutting" behavior. Cliffhanger: Why is Donovan breaking the queuing rules?

**Chapter 18 — The Chain of References**
Vera finally traces the obscured reference chain endpoint — it points not to anywhere in the Heap but to a corrupted type definition in the Metadata Attic. This definition holds references to all leaked objects, forming an unbreakable chain. Technical embodiment: GC Roots, reachability analysis, strong references preventing GC. Cliffhanger: Who wrote this corrupted type definition?

**Chapter 19 — Ambush in Stack Alley**
Cole is ambushed in Stack Alley. The attacker exploits thread stack mechanics — pushing recursive calls to fill his stack space, attempting a stack overflow. Jace arrives just in time, using CAS to replace the attacker's control variable and break the recursion. Technical embodiment: StackOverflowError, recursive stack pushing, stack frame overflow. Cliffhanger: How did the attacker know Cole's stack size?

**Chapter 20 — Double-Checked**
Cole and Jace need to access a critical facility using double-checked locking. Cole discovers the mechanism has been exploited — the time gap between two checks was leveraged. Technical embodiment: Double-checked locking (DCL), instruction reordering causing DCL failure, happen-before rules. Cliffhanger: Who exploited this vulnerability?

### Part Three: The Class Loading War (Chapters 21–30)

**Chapter 21 — Only One at a Time**
Cole locks down the critical passage to the Metadata Attic with a mutual exclusion lock — one thread folk at a time. But Donovan's forces try to force entry through the read-write lock's read passage. Cole anticipated this, placing both read and write locks at key nodes. Technical embodiment: Synchronized mutual exclusion, read-write lock application scenarios. Cliffhanger: What does Donovan want from Meta?

**Chapter 22 — A Hundred Paths Open at Once**
Nova uses NIO to build a parallel communications network for Cole — 120 channels operating simultaneously. They gain absolute advantage in the information war. But Nova warns: parallel doesn't mean synchronized; message ordering can't be guaranteed. Technical embodiment: NIO multiplexing, parallel processing vs. ordering conflict, Selector event-driven. Cliffhanger: What problems will out-of-order information cause?

**Chapter 23 — Not Enough Workers**
Slater's thread pool is in crisis — all core workers busy, task queue stretched to the horizon. He keeps hiring temps, but they're unreliable. Worse, when a temp crashes, its held resources aren't released. Technical embodiment: Thread pool saturation, unbounded queue OOM risk, thread leak causing resource non-release. Cliffhanger: Will Slater's thread pool collapse?

**Chapter 24 — The Forgotten Memory**
Meta reveals the final truth — Donovan tampered with class loading to inject malicious type definitions. These types create massive objects with strong references on initialization, forming unbreakable chains. Worse, they're embedded in the old generation, beyond normal young generation sweeps. Technical embodiment: Class loader tampering, malicious type strong references, metadata area-heap interaction. Cliffhanger: How to clear these protected malicious types?

**Chapter 25 — The Sweeper's Choice**
Warden faces a dilemma — to clear the malicious objects, he'd need to trigger a city-wide Full GC Pause. But that means freezing everyone, possibly for an excruciatingly long time. Cole asks for one hour to solve the root problem instead. Technical embodiment: Full GC cost, mark-compact algorithm, GC tuning trade-offs. Cliffhanger: Is one hour enough?

**Chapter 26 — Segmented Counterattack**
Grant deploys his segmented containers at key positions, dividing areas that previously needed city-wide locks into sixteen independent segments. Cole's team can clean multiple segments simultaneously without blocking each other — efficiency multiplies. Technical embodiment: ConcurrentHashMap segmented design, fine-grained locks improving concurrency, global-to-segmented lock evolution. Cliffhanger: Can segmented cleanup beat the clock?

**Chapter 27 — Fair and Unfair**
Quincy makes a difficult decision — temporarily disabling fair mode for the final battle. This means queued thread folk might be overtaken by newcomers. Unfair mode is brutal, but at least gives everyone a fighting chance. Technical embodiment: Fair vs. unfair lock throughput differences, fairness trade-offs. Cliffhanger: Will unfair mode cause greater chaos?

**Chapter 28 — Old Lock, New Lock**
Cole completes a perfect lock upgrade on the final passage to the Metadata Attic — from biased lock to lightweight lock to heavyweight lock. As competition intensifies, his locks upgrade accordingly, ultimately stopping Donovan's last attackers under maximum-security heavyweight control. Technical embodiment: Full lock upgrade process (biased → lightweight → heavyweight), lock selection under different contention levels. Cliffhanger: After upgrading, can locks downgrade again?

**Chapter 29 — The Types That Were Cleared**
Cole and Jace storm the Metadata Attic. Meta helps them initiate class unloading — purging the corrupted type definitions. But unloading requires the class loader itself to be reclaimed first. Cole must destroy Donovan's illegal loader with the Sweepers' help before the malicious types can be cleared. Technical embodiment: Class unloading conditions (class loader reclaimed), metadata area GC, parent-delegation model repair. Cliffhanger: Will class unloading damage useful types?

**Chapter 30 — After the Pause**
Malicious types cleared, reference chains broken. The Sweepers launch a gentle concurrent mark-sweep, reclaiming massive space. Pause times return to normal. Cole stands at the top of the Heap, watching the city resume. Jace jokes that Lockhaven needs better rules. Cole is quiet for a moment, then says: "The rules aren't the problem. The problem is people always want to bypass them." In the distance, Nova's 120 channels all flash green. Technical embodiment: Concurrent GC recovery, post-GC-tuning normal operation, system recovery. Cliffhanger: Are Lockhaven's rules truly safe? (Open ending)

## Writing Style

- **Language**: Hard cyberpunk, short sentences, fast pacing. No technical term explanations — let scenes and actions reveal the mechanisms.
- **Narrative POV**: Primarily from Cole's perspective, supplemented by Jace, Vera, and others.
- **Dialogue**: Short and punchy. No jargon dumping. Each character's speech matches their personality.
- **Technical Integration**: Concepts become city infrastructure and character abilities. No direct technical terms. Readers feel the technology through characters' actions and experiences.
- **Tone**: Tense but not oppressive, occasionally humorous. Jace's flippancy contrasts with Cole's silence.
- **Pacing**: Each chapter has a complete story arc (setup → development → twist), ending with a cliffhanger.
